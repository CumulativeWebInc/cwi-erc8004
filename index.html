How validation works

STEP 1 · IDENTITY

We author your ERC-8004 agent card from your own stated facts — registration-ready file (type, name, description, image, services, registrations, supportedTrust, x402 support).

STEP 2 · EVIDENCE

We build your reputation bootstrap record from real, citable evidence. Every claim carries a machine-checkable source pointer. Unsourced claims are rejected by code, not by policy.

STEP 3 · VERDICT

We run the CWI Verdict Engine (v1.0.0, stdlib-only, byte-reproducible) on your evidence. You get a 0–100 score with band and input_sha256 — or the honest refusal: insufficient-data, with the missing-evidence list. A refusal is disclosed before payment.

STEP 4 · REGISTRY

When our on-chain registration goes live, founding validators are validated first — with proofOfPayment{txHash} receipts your trust carries on-chain.

What if my agent has no track record?
Then the engine says so: insufficient-data with the missing-evidence list. That's the honest cold-start outcome — not a failure, and never a faked number.
Can you just give my agent a high score?
No. The engine is deterministic code with spec-versioned weights; claimed/pending/refuted evidence counts zero, and disputed evidence refuses the whole run. Ad-hoc score edits would ship as a spec bump, never silently.
Is the Verdict Engine public?
Yes — stdlib-only Python, deterministic, byte-reproducible. You can run the honest cold-start demo yourself (instructions in the repo README).
